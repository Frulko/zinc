## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
examples/pinball3d runs a table file: the 3D scene is built from the table (playfield, walls extruded, elements as meshes, the ball as a lit sphere, lights that switch), a fixed camera looking down a vertical table as the 90s desktop tables did; two layouts chosen at launch and described in the table file: landscape (table at the left, score panel at the right, 1280x800) and portrait (the whole table on a vertical screen, score in the backglass area); physics by zinc:physics3d (Jolt) at a fixed step: a real sphere on a tilted table, with ramps, rails and upper levels as volumes; flippers, plunger, nudge with tilt; scoring, missions and ranks from the rules data; attract mode; keyboard and touch input. Ships one original table in that spirit. Runs in the interpreter and AOT on the GPU renderer and headless on the software one. (Owner request, 2026-10-10: a pinball all in real 3D instead of fake 3D, with a suited physics engine, a vertical table in the style of Space Cadet, parametrable in its lanes and assets, with a complete editor to make new tables, visual and sound.)

Decisions
- Flippers and plunger are kinematic bodies moved by the game, not a hinge and a slider with motors (the coordinator left the call). Why: the stroke is exact and the same on every run; a flipper's strength is one number, its speed; no joint softness at the end stops; and zinc:physics3d's measurements say what matters is the step: at 960 steps a second the blade moves 0.3 cm a step. Hinge and slider exist in the plugin for a table that wants a free-swinging part.
- The board lies flat and gravity leans by the slope: geometry stays axis-aligned for the scene and the physics.
- Switches are lines, tested in the game against each ball's move in a step (rollovers, spinners, gates, the mouth and the end of a ramp, with the level they are on): Jolt's sensors see a ball only where a step ends. Kicker holes are tested the same way.
- A one-way gate is a wall that opens while a ball is close on the side it may come from.
- Locks are counted, not held: the dock throws the ball back and the third lock serves two more balls. Simple, and no ball waits in a hole.
- A ball that lies still for 5 s out of the plunger lane is served again ("ball search"): a ball that jumped over a wall cannot end a game.
- A cleared bank of drop targets comes back 0.6 s later and never under a ball (raised at once, the bank scored again and again from the ball it pushed).
- Static geometry is one mesh per look (15 draws for the table), moving things one node each.
- Text sizes of a table are rounded to 18 baked sizes (the engine bakes the sizes a program names).
- High scores are kept by zinc:storage per table name, never in deterministic runs.
- Format additions since ZN-649.01 (still version 1, no table exists outside the repository): ramps[].skirt (a solid ramp closed underneath where a ball would wedge: found when a ball stuck under the ramp's low end), rules.rankLights, rules.ballSaveLight, rules.multiball.lockLights. Tidal Lock retuned: smoother ramp profile (a kink at 10 degrees threw a 3.3 m/s ball over the ramp's wall), rail end at 3.4 cm, glass at 9.5, looks, cameras, high scores in both panels.
