## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
(Owner's answers, 2026-10-10, for the showcase demos of M27.) The table records, locally and without anything that identifies a visitor, what was seen: sessions (from first touch to idle reset), sequences opened and for how long, media opened, the language, presentations played to the end; a daily file the museum can read or export, and a summary screen behind a staff gesture.

## Implementation Notes

<!-- SECTION:NOTES:BEGIN -->
Done 2026-10-11 (parallel agent, third task of its batch). Tests, with the worktree's own build of 51d4809e: `PASS museum_stats` (15 s, interpreter and compiled), `PASS museum_mode`, `PASS museum_video`, `PASS museum_editor` (the editor imports the player).

What was built (examples/museum-table/src):
- `stats.ts` (new): `Counts` (a count and seconds by what is counted and its id, read from and written to the CSV of a day) and `Statistics` (the session under way: `begin`, `note`, `pass`, `touched`, `end`, `drop`, `today`). It opens no file itself: main.ts gives it the function that reads the file of a day and the one that writes it.
- `player.ts`: `counting` (null: nothing is counted). A session begins with the first touch of a visit and is added to its day at the reset that forgets the visit. Counted: the sequences opened and the seconds on each, the pictures seen in full, the audio descriptions asked for, the points of interest opened (by a touch on them or by "Next point"), the presentations started and played to their end, the hour the visit began in, the language it ended in. The seconds stop at the last touch, or run while a presentation is watched; the attract loop is not a visit.
- The staff's summary (`staff`, `drawSummary`): two fingers held three seconds within 40 units of two opposite corners show the counts of the day over the table, at the designed text size; the session those touches began is dropped; the next touch closes the summary and resets the table. A reset closes it too, so it never stays on a table nobody attends.
- `main.ts`: `--statistics <directory>`; the file of a day is `<directory>/YYYY-MM-DD.csv`. A write that fails is said on the error output and changes nothing on the table.
- Docs: "The statistics of a table" in docs/museum-table-format.md (the file, line by line), "Statistics" in the README.

The criteria, on the output of tests/t1/museum_stats.sh (the sample with a reset after 5 s and a visit of two stages, so that two visits, a presentation watched to its end and the staff's gesture fit in 5960 frames without a 3D sequence in the long part):
- #1: the file written equals golden/museum-stats/day.csv (two sessions, 81 s; the hour; fr and en; three sequences; the print in full; its description; the point `vase/lady`; the presentation started and ended); compiled, the same session writes the same file.
- #2: checked on the file by awk: the header, whole numbers only, seconds only on `session` and `sequence`, as many hours as sessions and each of two digits, nothing that looks like a time or a fraction; and none of the coordinates of the scripted touches is in it. The file after the staff's gesture equals the file before it.
- #3: golden/museum-stats/frames.txt, frame 5895: the summary with the counts of the two visits (looked at: 2 visits, 0 min 41 s, each sequence with its count and time, the print, the description, the point, the presentation 1 × to the end 1 ×).

Decisions (for the coordinator to record):
- A day is a CSV of sums (`what,count,seconds,id`), not JSON: a spreadsheet opens it, the id comes last so it may hold a comma, and a parsed JSON object cannot list its keys in the Zinc subset. No list of sessions is kept, only sums: nothing lets a visit be followed.
- The only time of day is the hour a visit began in; a visit belongs to the day it began on.
- Nothing is counted without `--statistics <directory>`: a test or a developer's run writes nowhere by default, and the museum chooses a place it can read.
- The staff's gesture is two fingers in two opposite corners for three seconds: no control to find on the table, hard to do by accident, and the corners hold no button.
- The time looked at stops at the last touch: the 120 seconds a table waits before it forgets a visit are not attention.

Not proven: a real day (the date and the hour come from the clock of the machine; the test runs at the epoch in UTC); midnight during a visit (it is added to the day it began on); a disk that is full (the write is in a try, not tested).

Limits: a visit under way when the table is switched off is not counted. The summary shows today only.
