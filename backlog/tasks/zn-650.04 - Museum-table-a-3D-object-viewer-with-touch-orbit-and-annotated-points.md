## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
An object of the collection as a textured 3D model on the GPU renderer of zinc:3d: one finger turns it, pinch zooms, two fingers move it, with inertia and limits; points of interest on the model open their description and move the camera to them; lighting presets; a reset. Models load from the assets of the project (OBJ today; glTF if zinc:3d can load it, else a proposed follow-up). (Owner request, 2026-10-10: a demo app useful for mediation in museums: an interactive presentation on a touch table, photo gestures, 3D models, descriptions, presentations, in the manner of Klynt.)
<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:END -->

## Implementation Notes

<!-- SECTION:NOTES:BEGIN -->
Done (parallel worktree, 2026-10-10). Test: `next/tests/run --tier t1 --only museum_viewer` -> PASS museum_viewer; museum_panorama, museum_table, museum_format, museum_assets PASS with it.

What exists
- examples/museum-table/src/viewer.ts: ObjectView. The model of a sequence's `model` item is drawn by zinc:3d in the item's box: the object turns about its centre (one finger, 0.35 degree per table unit, with inertia), comes closer (pinch, 1.3 to 6 radii), moves with two fingers (up to 0.8 radius), under lights that stay with the camera. Points of interest are projected every frame from the same rotation and camera: a ring on each one that faces the camera; a touch opens its description (title and text on a card), glides the view to the point's own view in 0.6 s and puts the object beside the card; a touch elsewhere closes it. Two buttons in the box: the lighting (the three presets of the format: studio, raking, flat) and the view of departure (a glide back). The object's label (title, creator, date, medium) is in the box.
- src/orbit.ts: Orbit, the turning, inertia, pinch and glide shared with the panorama: src/panorama.ts (the main loop's PanoramaView) now extends it, with the same arithmetic (museum_panorama's frames and log are unchanged).
- src/main.ts: the viewer is created for the first `.obj` model of the sequence; cards lie over it and take their fingers first; the wheel and the trackpad zoom it; `--log` adds the view and the position of every point, `--pins` puts a red ball on each point of the object, `--time` prints the draw time.
- The sample: the vase has five points of interest placed from the decoration the model shows (the court lady in her pavilion, the dancer on her carpet, the musicians and their drum, the flowers of the neck, the cobalt of the plantain), each with its view; the opening view is the whole vase.
- next/tests/t1/museum_viewer.sh, tests/data/museum_viewer/ (session.py writes session.input; expected.log), tests/golden/museum-viewer/frames.txt.

Acceptance criteria
- #1 scripted session (turn, zoom, open a point, reset; also move and lighting): frames 75, 130 and 205 hash to tests/golden/museum-viewer/frames.txt (112107d9cea562ab, 3af100d85f589635, 3266a958bad9f595) on the software renderer, with `--interp` and compiled, and the 814-line view log equals expected.log on both. GPU renderer of zinc:3d (`ZINC_3D=gpu`) at frame 130 against the software frame: 2164 pixels of 2 073 600 over 24 (0.10 percent), mean error 0.11; the test allows 3 percent.
- #2 a point stays on its place: the log gives every point's screen position at every change of view (expected.log); with `--pins` the red ball that is part of the object is found within 4 pixels of that position for every point facing the camera at four views (frames 35, 75, 130, 190: a quarter turn, zoomed and moved, glided to a point, back at the start). Points on the far side are not drawn.
- #3 frame time with the sample model (12 000 triangles, 1536-pixel texture, a 1000 x 810 pixel box, machine loaded: load average 11): software renderer 4.5 ms per draw at the opening view, 5.5 ms zoomed in; GPU renderer 0.7 ms and 1.15 ms (headless, `--time`, `ZINC_3D=software|gpu`). Loading the OBJ and its texture at the start of the sequence is within the 0.9 s start of the player.

Decisions
- The object turns and the camera and lights stay (an object held under studio light), rather than a camera walking round a lit scene: every side is seen well lit, and the raking preset rakes whatever is turned to the front. A view's yaw and pitch are written as the camera's place around the object (yaw 0 in front of +z, 90 on the +x side), distance in radii: independent of the model's scale.
- Field of view 40 degrees: at the format's default distance of 3.2 radii any object is whole in the box.
- "Facing the camera" is judged from the centre of the object (right for a vase, a bust): zinc:3d gives no depth back. A hollow or folded object needs the depth of the frame: proposed follow-up.
- glTF and GLB (owner's point 4; zinc:3d/gltf, ZN-658, arrived on main during the task): the viewer takes a `.glb` or `.gltf` model as well as an OBJ (loadGltf; the box around the model comes from the bounds its POSITION accessors declare). Checked by hand with the Smithsonian's own file of the vase, `f1980_191-150k-4096.glb` (5.9 MB, 150 000 triangles in one mesh of 76 801 vertices, a 4096-pixel JPEG, no extension required): it loads and plays the test session. It gives the better frame (finer texture, smooth silhouette, none of the ripples the decimation leaves in the texture coordinates) at 1.38 ms per draw on the GPU renderer and 20.9 ms on the software one (OBJ: 0.7 and 4.5 ms), and the player starts 0.9 s later. The OBJ is what ships: with the GLB the example would weigh 11.4 MB against the 8 MB limit (7.1 MB today). No automated test of the GLB path, since no GLB ships: see the first follow-up.
- A slight specular highlight (0x262626, exponent 48) stands for the glaze; per vertex in software, per pixel on the GPU, within the tolerance above.

Limits (ponytail comments in the code)
- One model per sequence; a glTF made of placed parts needs the bounds of the loaded node from zinc:3d (the nodes' transforms are not applied to the box); orientation toward the visitor's side and the text-size setting come with ZN-650.06 and .10.

Proposed follow-ups (not created)
- Sample, size S: ship the vase as a decimated GLB instead of the OBJ: tools/decimate.cpp writes a binary GLB (about 30 000 triangles and the 1536-pixel texture in about 1.3 MB, less than today's 1.6 MB of OBJ and JPEG, with 2.5 times the triangles), the project names it, museum_viewer's goldens are recorded again and then cover the GLB path. AC: the example stays under 8 MB; museum_viewer passes on the GLB; the silhouette of the vase shows no facet at the opening view.
- zinc:3d, size S: the bounds of a Node (loadGltf's result), so that a viewer frames any model.
- zinc:3d, size S: give back the depth at a pixel (or a visibility query for a point), so that points of interest hide behind any shape. AC: a point behind a handle is not drawn.
- Player, size S: several models in one sequence.
