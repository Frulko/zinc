## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
The Klynt part: a sequence shows its media and texts in its layout, with timed appearances; hotspots and links lead to other sequences with a transition; a storyboard view (the map of all sequences and their links) to jump anywhere and see where one has been; a guided presentation mode that plays a path of sequences by itself with narration (zinc:audio, ZN-390) and subtitles, which a touch pauses; a language switch. (Owner request, 2026-10-10: a demo app useful for mediation in museums: an interactive presentation on a touch table, photo gestures, 3D models, descriptions, presentations, in the manner of Klynt.)

Owner's answers, 2026-10-10: narration is a synthesised voice generated from the texts with the Mac's say command, in French and English, replaceable by a recording; every narration is subtitled.

Owner's second answer, 2026-10-10: the macOS licence is reported to forbid redistributing recordings of the `say` voices (to verify), so nothing generated by `say` is committed. The narration uses an open-source synthesis (Piper or an equivalent) with one French and one English voice whose licences allow distribution, each licence checked on its own page and recorded in LICENSES; the audio files are generated by a script kept in the example and shipped with the demo.

## Implementation Notes

2026-10-10, slices 1 and 2 (timed items and next; links, hotspots, overlays, transitions). The player is now a stack of stages (src/stage.ts: one sequence on the table with its clock, cards, 3D object, panorama and link buttons) under main.ts, which owns the story: go(), the overlay, the transitions, next. Decisions: (1) an overlay is closed by a link or a next of the overlay that names the sequence underneath, found as it was left (no close button of its own while the sample's overlay has its link back; a second overlay replaces the first); (2) the sequence under an overlay is dimmed, its clock stopped, and gives up its title and buttons; (3) next waits until no finger is on the table, so a visitor holding a card is not cut off; (4) fade is a dip to the table colour (0.5 s, works for the 3D views too), slide draws both sequences (0.45 s); nothing answers to a touch during a transition; (5) hotspot fractions are of the picture for a photograph or a film and of the box for a model, a panorama, a text panel; a hotspot on a card lives in table.ts (it moves with the card), one on a view is a round button of the stage; (6) the panorama is embedded as a window (src/lookout.ts on PanoramaView); the button of a point with to is a link with a fade, in the object viewer too (shared src/panel.ts); (7) 3D views are kept by medium id and reset when their sequence comes back, so an attract loop does not load the model again; (8) the texts and buttons are laid out in the visitor's frame (src/frame.ts), which is the table itself until ZN-650.06 turns it; (9) the language button moved from the top right corner to the visitor's edge (bottom right): every control of the player is now at the edge the visitor stands at. 2026-10-10, slice 3 (the storyboard). src/storyboard.ts draws the editor's picture for the visitor: nodes of 240 by 150 units at storyboard x, y (their centre), fitted to the visitor's frame (scale at most 1.7), each a cream card with a miniature of its table (the photographs themselves, a paper box for a text, an outlined shape for the object) and its title; arrows with the editor's bend (editor/storyboard.tsx route()), dashed for an overlay, one per pair of sequences, for links, next and points of interest with to. The trail: a tick on every sequence seen, the links followed in the accent colour, the current sequence framed with 'You are here'. A touch on a node goes there with a fade from wherever the visitor is (an overlay closes), a touch beside closes the map; the sequence underneath stops its clock while the map is open. Decision: a jump made from the storyboard has no arrow, so it shows by its tick only. The button 'Plan' / 'Map' is at the left of the visitor's edge. Miniatures ignore the rotation of the items (a second matrix under the frame's is not composed).

2026-10-10, slice 4 (presentations, narration, subtitles), and the end of the task.
Narration (decision, after the owner's second answer): Piper 2023.11.14-2 with two voices trained from scratch on open data, fr_FR-mls-medium speaker 1840 (Multilingual LibriSpeech, CC BY 4.0) and en_GB-cori-medium (LibriVox recordings, public domain), both from huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices (MIT); each model card read on 2026-10-10. Left aside because unclear: every voice 'finetuned from' en_US-lessac (fr_FR-siwis-medium, fr_FR-upmc-medium, en_GB-alba...: the Lessac data of Blizzard 2013 is licensed to a named licensee), the voices tuned from en_US-ryan (CC BY-NC-SA: fr_FR-siwis-low, gilles-low, mls_1840-low), fr_FR-tom (AGPL). tools/narration.sh downloads Piper and the voices to ~/.cache/zinc-museum-tts (190 MB, nothing in the repository; the macOS archive of Piper lacks its libraries, taken from the piper-phonemize release), synthesises each sentence on its own so the WebVTT cues are exact, and encodes MP3 with libmp3lame -q:a 7 (mono 22.05 kHz, 4 kB/s; the machine's ffmpeg has no libvorbis, its own vorbis encoder is experimental). 8 clips, 533 kB; the example is 7.5 MB (7 879 224 bytes); museum_assets now allows 12 MB. zinc:audio decodes the 8 clips (ZINC_NATIVE=real, durations equal to the last cue to 1 ms). Sample: narrations written for japonisme, porcelain and garden; wave has none (the film will bring its own in ZN-650.08).
Player decisions: (1) a narration is said once per visit outside a presentation (the first sequence's waits for the first touch), every time in a presentation; it stops when the sequence is left; (2) its length is the end of its last cue, not the decoded sound, so a silent or deterministic run shows the same subtitles at the same frames; without a WebVTT file the transcript's sentences are shown at 15 characters a second; (3) a language change restarts the narration and its subtitles in the new language; (4) a presentation step lasts the sequence's duration, else its narration plus a second, else 10 s; fade between steps; at the end the visitor stays on the last sequence; (5) while it plays the links are not offered and any touch only pauses it; paused, the sequence is free, 'Resume' plays the step again from its beginning because zinc:audio cannot pause or seek (follow-up below), a link or a jump leaves the presentation; (6) each presentation has its button in the row at the visitor's edge; (7) a text card is measured again when the language changes (it was cut when the other language was longer).
