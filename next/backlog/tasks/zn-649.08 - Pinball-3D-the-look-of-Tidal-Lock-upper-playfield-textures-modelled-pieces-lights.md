## Implementation Notes

<!-- SECTION:NOTES:BEGIN -->
Done 2026-10-10 in a parallel worktree (branch worktree-agent-a52f6ccde0b6b258d). usage: n/a (a parallel agent). Screenshots: before docs/img/pinball3d-{play,attract,portrait}.png (the first playable version, kept), after docs/img/pinball3d-look-{play,attract,portrait}.jpg (GPU renderer, headless).

Slices, each looked at on a screenshot before the next
1. Images of looks. A look's `texture` is drawn: the image of the table's folder (zinc:gfx imageFromBytes, PNG or JPEG) laid flat over each element as the top view shows it (geometry.ts Solid.drape: its corners at the corners of the box around the element); a model keeps its mesh's mapping. Tidal Lock: the painting of the playfield (space, the gas giant, the station's hull, lanes, legends), the ramp's chevrons, the bumper caps, the flippers, the ball, the planet in the corner.
2. Lights. A light with a `glow` throws a pool of light: a disc of the playfield, a hair above it, that shows the look's `textureLit` (the same painting with every light lit, which the art script fades to the plain one at the rim of each pool, so no edge shows and no transparency is needed). `lighting.general` names the general illumination: the scene's lamps and ambient fall to `dimmed` while those lights are off (rules: on at the start of a game and of a ball, off at a tilt and when the game is over). A bumper, slingshot or target also shows its look's glow while its `light` is lit (the bumpers pulse with the mission's light). Each panel has an `image` (the backglass).
3. Modelled pieces. The model path runs: the art script writes OBJ files (station.obj 256 faces, tug.obj 68, two plates of 5), scene.ts loads them through zinc:3d parseObj and physics.ts takes their hull. A model that collides is an element like another: rules hear its `hit`, and it may name its sound. Cabinet rails, lock bar, apron and backboard are boxes with an image.
4. The upper playfield. The control deck, 5.5 cm above the board in the upper right: the dock no longer throws the ball, it lifts it (kicker `exit` [-2.4, -18.3, 5.5]); the ball rolls to the deck's own flipper (left button), which shoots two beacons (both lit: 15,000 and one more bonus multiplier) and the station's tower (a mesh with a hull, 750); left alone it leaves by the chute, falls back on the playfield and is turned by the two relays (the standups, now set across the right lane) into the right inlane.
5. The GPU renderer is what a window uses (zinc:3d's `renderer: auto`, 4 samples a pixel, mipmaps); the software frames stay the goldens.

To take again on a quiet machine.
- Art: 14 images and 4 models, 679,102 bytes (the two playfield paintings are 399 KB of it). With the sounds the table's folder is 1.3 MB.

Decisions
- Pools of light without transparency: the renderers of zinc:3d have neither blending nor a per-piece light, so a pool is an opaque patch of a second, lit painting that equals the first at the pool's rim. It costs one more image (208 KB) and asks that pools do not overlap (the art script warns). An engine with additive blending would do it with one image: proposed below.
- Images are laid flat from above, one rule for every generated shape; a wall or a target's face cannot carry one. Enough for a pinball seen from above; per-face mapping is a later need.
- Upper-level bodies are made last in physics.ts, so that the playfield plays the same with or without an upper playfield.
- The standups were moved, not a guide added: a ball coming down the right lane used to balance on the top edge of a standup (a trap that existed before, found by the deck's chute); set across the lane at 226 degrees they turn it into the inlane (24 variants probed, this one sends all 18 probes of its neighbourhood to the inlane). Moving them does not change the scripted log.
- The deck drains to one chute: in the first layout a ball that went under the raised flipper fell off the deck's open edge into the dock again, which counted a lock each time; the space under the flipper now leads to the chute, whose way out is past the dock. The deck's walls reach the glass (3.9 cm) because a flipped ball hops (8.2 cm at its highest in a probe, the deck being at 5.5).
- Models' colours come from a 64x64 palette image through their texture coordinates: one look for every modelled piece.
- Format (version 1, optional fields): look.textureLit, light.glow, bumper.light, slingshot.light, lighting.general, lighting.dimmed, panel.image, model.sounds.hit; roles `offset` (kicker.exit) and `area` (floor.points) for the editor. src/images.ts keeps the images read per table source, so an editor that rebuilds the scene does not use up the engine's 64 images (ZN-670); forgetImages() frees them.
