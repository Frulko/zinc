## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
The Klynt part: a sequence shows its media and texts in its layout, with timed appearances; hotspots and links lead to other sequences with a transition; a storyboard view (the map of all sequences and their links) to jump anywhere and see where one has been; a guided presentation mode that plays a path of sequences by itself with narration (zinc:audio, ZN-390) and subtitles, which a touch pauses; a language switch. (Owner request, 2026-10-10: a demo app useful for mediation in museums: an interactive presentation on a touch table, photo gestures, 3D models, descriptions, presentations, in the manner of Klynt.)

Owner's answers, 2026-10-10: narration is a synthesised voice generated from the texts with the Mac's say command, in French and English, replaceable by a recording; every narration is subtitled.

Owner's second answer, 2026-10-10: the macOS licence is reported to forbid redistributing recordings of the `say` voices (to verify), so nothing generated by `say` is committed. The narration uses an open-source synthesis (Piper or an equivalent) with one French and one English voice whose licences allow distribution, each licence checked on its own page and recorded in LICENSES; the audio files are generated by a script kept in the example and shipped with the demo.

## Implementation Notes

2026-10-10, slices 1 and 2 (timed items and next; links, hotspots, overlays, transitions). The player is now a stack of stages (src/stage.ts: one sequence on the table with its clock, cards, 3D object, panorama and link buttons) under main.ts, which owns the story: go(), the overlay, the transitions, next. Decisions: (1) an overlay is closed by a link or a next of the overlay that names the sequence underneath, found as it was left (no close button of its own while the sample's overlay has its link back; a second overlay replaces the first); (2) the sequence under an overlay is dimmed, its clock stopped, and gives up its title and buttons; (3) next waits until no finger is on the table, so a visitor holding a card is not cut off; (4) fade is a dip to the table colour (0.5 s, works for the 3D views too), slide draws both sequences (0.45 s); nothing answers to a touch during a transition; (5) hotspot fractions are of the picture for a photograph or a film and of the box for a model, a panorama, a text panel; a hotspot on a card lives in table.ts (it moves with the card), one on a view is a round button of the stage; (6) the panorama is embedded as a window (src/lookout.ts on PanoramaView); the button of a point with to is a link with a fade, in the object viewer too (shared src/panel.ts); (7) 3D views are kept by medium id and reset when their sequence comes back, so an attract loop does not load the model again; (8) the texts and buttons are laid out in the visitor's frame (src/frame.ts), which is the table itself until ZN-650.06 turns it; (9) the language button moved from the top right corner to the visitor's edge (bottom right): every control of the player is now at the edge the visitor stands at. Test: next/tests/t1/museum_visit.sh (story log + 6 frame hashes, interpreter and compiled; wait.input: next after 30 s). Goldens of museum_table and museum_viewer re-recorded (language button moved, hotspot on the Great Wave, the text card now comes at 2 s); their logs are unchanged. affected.json: add museum_visit to the museum-table mapping, and tests/t1/museum_visit.sh + tests/data/museum_visit/ + tests/golden/museum-visit/ as paths.

2026-10-10, slice 3 (the storyboard). src/storyboard.ts draws the editor's picture for the visitor: nodes of 240 by 150 units at storyboard x, y (their centre), fitted to the visitor's frame (scale at most 1.7), each a cream card with a miniature of its table (the photographs themselves, a paper box for a text, an outlined shape for the object) and its title; arrows with the editor's bend (editor/storyboard.tsx route()), dashed for an overlay, one per pair of sequences, for links, next and points of interest with to. The trail: a tick on every sequence seen, the links followed in the accent colour, the current sequence framed with 'You are here'. A touch on a node goes there with a fade from wherever the visitor is (an overlay closes), a touch beside closes the map; the sequence underneath stops its clock while the map is open. Decision: a jump made from the storyboard has no arrow, so it shows by its tick only. The button 'Plan' / 'Map' is at the left of the visitor's edge. Miniatures ignore the rotation of the items (a second matrix under the frame's is not composed).
