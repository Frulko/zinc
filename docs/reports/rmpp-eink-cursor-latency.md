# rMPP sidecar: moving pointer and update latency on e-ink (research, 2026-10-28)

Follows `rmpp-eink-monitor-research.md` (not repeated: Modos per-pixel scheduling, Mira/Dasung presets, dithering). Focus: the pointer and the update path through xochitl.
Convention: (S) stated by a source, (I) our inference, (U) not found. **The public record is thin**: nothing documents the Paper Pro (Gallery 3) refresh-mode enum, qtfb's modes, or any e-ink cursor technique beyond the points below. Most items under "Ranked" are therefore experiments, not known fixes.

## What the sources say

### xochitl, rm2fb and the pen path
- (S) rM2 has no hardware TCON: xochitl drives the panel from software ("SWTCON", `libqsgepaper`), loads waveforms from disk and renders on a separate thread. rm2fb reimplements it ([rmkit status](https://rmkit.dev/news/rm2-status/), [timower/rM2-stuff](https://github.com/timower/rM2-stuff): "reverse engineered software TCON based on libqsgepaper 3.23", tested to 3.28 beta; [ddvk/remarkable2-framebuffer](https://github.com/ddvk/remarkable2-framebuffer): its README lists "understanding the waveforms used by SWTCON" as an open task). [libqsgepaper-snoop](https://github.com/pl-semiotics/libqsgepaper-snoop) shows the hook point: `EPFramebuffer::sendUpdate` gets a damage region from a memfd-backed framebuffer. No source gives the rM2 or rMPP frame rate or per-mode waveform lengths.
- (S) Marker latency is advertised at 21 ms (rM1: 55 ms), attributed to per-pixel waveform driving ([Liliputing](https://liliputing.com/2020/03/remarkable-launches-a-2nd-gen-epaper-and-pen-tablet.html)). No source describes a pen-only fast path or prediction.
- (U) No public enum for the Paper Pro modes. qtfb is archived and merged into [rm-appload](https://github.com/asivery/rm-appload) (it is "a QPainter drawing a shared memory region", [asivery/qtfb](https://github.com/asivery/qtfb)); the READMEs do not list modes. `libremarkable` has a `spy-xochitl` target (LD_PRELOAD to log new refresh enums from xochitl calls), the documented way to recover them ([libremarkable](https://github.com/canselcik/libremarkable)).
- (I) A qtfb client cannot reach the pen's path: it can only submit a rect plus one of our five modes to xochitl's `sendUpdate`. UFAST is presumably the same waveform class the pen uses (a short 1-bit DU/A2-like drive), so UFAST on a small rect is the nearest equivalent. Names (I): UFAST ~ A2/DU-fast, FAST ~ DU, ANIMATE ~ a 4-level or 2-bit fast mode, CONTENT ~ GL16/GLR16, UI ~ GC16-class. Verify by measuring, not by name.

### Waveform classes (generic, for orientation)
- (S) A2: 1-bit, black/white only transitions, fastest, accumulates ghosting; DU: any grey to black/white; DU4: 4 levels; GL16: 16 levels, no flash, low ghosting; GC16: full, flashing ([IT8951 docs](https://unpkg.com/it8951@0.1.1/docs/enums/_it8951_.waveform.md), [E Ink patent US11380273](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11380273)). Repeated non-flashing updates accumulate ghosting until a flashing refresh ([same patent family](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11195480)). Old-panel numbers: A2 ~125 ms, GC16 ~600 ms; PineNote GC16 ~450 ms ([CVERInc/pinenote](https://github.com/CVERInc/pinenote)). Gallery 3 colour is slower than monochrome Carta (I: expect fast modes in the 100-250 ms range and CONTENT/UI at 400 ms to 1 s+; measure).
- (S) Partial updates cause **edge blooming**: stray fields from driven pixels shift neighbours that are not driven, at the rect boundary; E Ink's mitigation is to drive the neighbours with a paired waveform at the same time, or to enlarge the region so the edge falls in a place where it hides; the artifact clears within one or two later updates ([US11557260](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11557260)). (I) This is a likely part of our "ghost" around a 28 px pointer: a tight rect puts the bloom edge on visible white.

### Collisions and in-flight pixels
- (S) The Linux Rockchip EBC and i.MX paths schedule per update area; an update touching pixels still being driven must wait or restart them (Modos calls the restart "early cancellation", see earlier report). The PineNote driver adds an `auto_refresh` that counts accumulated area and flashes; on A2 the counter never trips, so the community turns it off and clears on idle (8 s in [CVERInc/pinenote](https://github.com/CVERInc/pinenote); the 8 s is theirs, not a recommendation). Pine64 notes the rk3566 driver does not support several waveforms at once ([RK3566 EBC page](https://wiki.pine64.org/wiki/RK3566_EBC_Reverse-Engineering)).
- (U) No source on xochitl's own collision rule (queue, merge, or restart). We must measure it.

### Cursor practice
- (S) Boox Mira: Speed preset gives a fluid cursor with more ghosting; Image preset makes the cursor "very slow" ([review](https://myereader.substack.com/p/onyx-boox-mira-review-features-neededhtml)). Modos: FPGA "special compensation" for the cursor, undisclosed ([deep dive](https://www.crowdsupply.com/modos-tech/modos-paper-monitor/updates/a-technical-deep-dive-into-glider)). Developers of e-ink drag UIs draw with a fast mode during the drag and a clean refresh on release (forum reports in a general search, no single citable source).
- (U) Nothing found on XOR/inverted cursors, balanced-transition sprites, or hide-while-moving in any e-ink product. The ideas below are ours.

## Why the cursor looks bad (I, from the symptoms)
1. **Trail**: a 1-bit sprite moved in a fast mode leaves partial-transition residue on the pixels it left (white to black to white too quickly) plus bloom at the old rect edge. The 150 ms black-then-white flash is itself two more fast updates on those pixels: it re-ghosts the neighbours and shows as a "refresh at an odd moment".
2. **Start-of-motion latency**: the first move costs a mode switch (~30 ms) if the stream was in CONTENT/UI, plus any in-flight update on the pointer's old/new tiles that must finish first.
3. **Idle flashes**: the clean fires on the pointer rect while other content is settling, so the user sees small flashes unrelated to their action.

## Ranked things to try (expected gain; all to be measured on the tablet)

1. **Stay in UFAST for the whole pointer session; never switch modes mid-motion.** Pointer-only rects in UFAST, and send the rest of the screen's changes in UFAST too while the pointer moves (or defer them). Removes the ~30 ms switch at motion start. Gain: first-move latency down by ~30 ms; no visible mode-change flicker. Cost: low (a hold-mode timer).
2. **One rect per pointer step covering old and new positions (union, padded 8-16 px), instead of two rects.** A single update drives old and new pixels in one waveform: no "erase then draw" ordering, fewer collisions, and the bloom edge is pushed away from the sprite. Gain: removes most of the trail behind a fast move; fewer updates per second. Cost: low.
3. **Rate-limit to the panel, not to the Mac: newest-wins, at most one in-flight update on the pointer region.** Measure the real fly time of a UFAST rect (send a rect, watch with a camera or photodiode, or time the next accepted update); then send pointer updates at that period, not at 120 Hz. Updates to pixels still in flight are the likely source of the "latency when it starts" and of smear. Gain: stable latency, less ghosting; this is the key measurement. Cost: low to medium.
4. **Predict the pointer position by one panel period (linear, 1-2 samples).** The pen does not need it; the Mac pointer does, because the panel adds 100+ ms. Draw where the pointer will be at the end of the update. Gain: perceived lag cut by up to one update period; risk: overshoot on stops (clamp, drop prediction when speed falls). Cost: low.
5. **Replace the delayed black-then-white flash with a paired clean: when the pointer leaves a rect, redraw the old rect once in CONTENT (or ANIMATE) with the underlying content, not a black/white flash.** A flash of both polarities in UFAST ghosts neighbours; a grey-capable mode returns pixels to their true level and is the nearest thing to Caster's "unchanged for a while, re-render in grey". Trigger it only after the pointer has been still for ~250-400 ms (not per pass) and only on the tiles it touched. Gain: no black/white flicker, cleaner residue; cost: one 400 ms+ update on a few tiles (do not move the pointer during it; if motion resumes, accept the collision). Cost: medium.
6. **Try ANIMATE for the pointer rect only.** It is documented nowhere; (I) 4-level fast mode with lower ghosting than UFAST. A/B on a test pattern: speed (frames until the sprite is fully dark) and residue after 50 passes. If ANIMATE is within ~1.5x of UFAST latency and ghosts less, use it for the pointer. Gain: unknown, potentially the best trade. Cost: low (a switch).
7. **Make the sprite friendlier to the waveform**: solid black arrow with a 1-2 px white halo, no thin white interior, no antialiasing, no pixels that are near the dither thresholds; alternatively invert the content under it (XOR) so the pointer is dark on light and light on dark and the rest is untouched. Fewer 1-px features means fewer partially driven pixels. Gain: less visible residue; cost: low. Keep the earlier report's "overlay, not content" rule.
8. **Move the clean off the pointer's tiles when other updates are in flight**: schedule it after the global idle, merged with the tile clean of the earlier report (its item 1), so one clean replaces the pointer flash and the 1 s pass. Gain: removes "refresh at odd moments". Cost: low.
9. **Hide-while-moving, draw-at-rest as a fallback mode (option, off by default)**: during fast motion draw a small 8-12 px dot (or nothing) and draw the full arrow once the speed drops under a threshold. A smaller sprite touches a quarter of the pixels. Gain: large cut in trail when moving fast; cost: looks less polished. Medium.
10. **Measure the mode table once and keep it**: a small tool that sends a 28 px sprite 50 times in each mode at 30/60/120 Hz and records fly time (camera at 240 fps) and residue (photo of the rect after 5 s). Also run `spy-xochitl`-style logging to see which mode and rect xochitl uses for the Marker (LD_PRELOAD on xochitl, as libremarkable does for ioctls; on the Paper Pro, hook the equivalent of `sendUpdate`). Gain: replaces guesses 1-9 with numbers; cost: medium, and it is the only way to learn the Gallery 3 timings.

Order to do first: 3 and 10 (measure), then 2, 1, 5, 6, 4; 7-9 as refinement.
