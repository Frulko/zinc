# ESP32-2432S022C: battery, buttons, free pins and an "off" mode

Researched 2026-10-22. Nothing here was measured on a board: everything comes from the vendor schematic V1.0 (read from two PNG sheets), datasheets and community sources. "Verified" means read off the schematic or a datasheet; "inferred" means a conclusion drawn from them; "not found" means no source exists in what I could reach.

## Where the schematic is

The vendor (Shenzhen Jingcai Intelligent, JCZN, title block "ESP32-2432S022 V1.0") schematic exists only as two PNG sheets, "LCM" (display, touch, ESP32, audio) and "MCU" (power, USB, charger, SD). I found them in a third-party repository, not on a vendor page: `assets/ESP32-2432S022-LCM-V1.0.png` and `assets/ESP32-2432S022-MCU-V1.0.png` in earlchirchir/ESP32-2432S022C-Capabilities-Showcase-Firmware. No PDF and no vendor download page was found (lcdwiki has no page for this model). Caveat: it is the V1.0 schematic of the "S022" family; a later hardware revision of the C board could differ (confidence that it matches the C board: high, because every net I could compare matches the repo's preset: LCD data 15/13/12/14/27/25/33/32, WR 4, RD 2, CS 17, DC 16, BL 0, I2C 21/22).

Note a conflict: the rzeldent Sunton board list calls the 2432S022C "SPI ST7789 + CST816S". The schematic and the repo preset show an 8-bit parallel bus (pin IM0 of the panel connector is tied to 3.3 V, DB0..DB15 are routed), so the list is wrong on the bus.

## 1. Battery

| Item | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Connector | P2, JST 1.25 mm 2-pin, nets BAT+ / BAT- | verified (schematic; rzeldent list also says "Battery interface JST 1.25 2p") |
| Charger / boost IC | U8, 8 pins + exposed pad named VIN, LED1, LED2, LED3, KEY, BAT, SW, VOUT: the IP5306 pinout (charger + 5 V boost, 3 charge LEDs, key). The part number is not printed on the drawing | pinout verified, "IP5306" inferred (high confidence). It is not a TP4054. A teardown of the sibling 2424S012C also names an IP5306 |
| Inductor and sense | L1 from SW to BAT+, R30/C24 network on the BAT pin, C25 across the cell | verified |
| Power path | USB VBUS goes through D1 (1N5819W) to the net "5V", into U8 VIN. U8 VOUT is the net VOUT-BAT, which feeds both 3.3 V regulators (U7 for the ESP32, U1 for the panel), each an AMS1117-3.3. So the whole board runs from the IP5306 output, also on USB | verified |
| Battery voltage to an ADC pin | None. BAT+ goes only to P2, L1, C25, R30 and U8. No divider exists. On the ESP32 sheet GPIO34, 35, 36 (VP) and 39 (VN) are drawn with a no-connect cross | verified (high). The firmware cannot read the battery voltage or the charge state; the three LED pins of U8 are left open (no LEDs drawn) |
| Power key | SW1, a push button, through R29 (about 100 ohm class, value not legible) to U8 KEY, to GND | verified; "this is the power on/off button of the IP5306" inferred (high) |
| P1 | 4-pin HC-1.25 header: VIN (through Q1 AO3401 P-MOSFET into the 5 V net), TXD2, RXD2 (to U0TXD/U0RXD through 100 ohm and 47k parts), GND | verified |

So: connector JST 1.25 2P, charger IP5306-class, no battery sense. If a battery gauge is wanted, the only way is a hardware change (a divider to a free pin; but no free pin is broken out, see section 3).

## 2. Buttons and what GPIO0 does

| Control | Wiring | Status |
|---|---|---|
| BOOT | Switch from GPIO0 to GND, with R2 10k pull-up to 3V3 | verified |
| RST | Switch from EN to GND (R1 10k pull-up and C4 on EN). Also tied to the net TFT_RST: the panel reset and the touch reset (R27, 0 ohm) are on EN, which is why the preset uses `rst: -1` | verified |
| Power button | SW1 on the IP5306 KEY pin (see above) | verified/inferred as above |
| Other user button, power slide switch | None drawn | verified (not on schematic) |
| CH340C auto-reset | NPN transistors T1/T2 on RTS/DTR drive IO0 and EN (the usual esptool circuit). Flashing therefore pulses GPIO0 | verified |

The backlight: LCD pins LED+ are tied to 3.3 V; the LED cathodes go through R11 (3.9 ohm) to the drain of Q2 (AO3402 N-MOSFET). The gate is the net JO0 (GPIO0) with R10 10k to GND. A high on GPIO0 lights the backlight. Verified.

What it means electrically (inferred, no measurement):

- BOOT pressed while the firmware runs: GPIO0 is shorted to GND by the switch. The backlight goes dark while the button is held (the FET gate is pulled low), and if the pin is an output driving high or a PWM, the pad sources its short-circuit current into the switch (a few tens of mA at most, the same as any "button on an output pin"; it survives in practice but it is not a clean design). The firmware cannot read the button while LEDC owns the pin, except by switching the pin to input between frames; that would flicker the backlight.
- At reset the pin is high impedance: R2 (10k up) and R10 (10k down) make a divider near 1.65 V, plus the ESP32 internal strapping pull-up (about 45k). That is between the strapping thresholds (roughly 0.25 and 0.75 of 3.3 V). Boards boot normally in practice (the working firmwares of the repos above rely on it), so I treat it as marginal but working; no source documents it.
- In deep sleep with GPIO0 not held, the same divider (about 1.65 V on the gate, above the AO3402 typical threshold of about 1 V, which I did not look up) may partly turn the backlight on. Inferred, medium-low confidence. Hold GPIO0 low in sleep (`rtc_gpio_set_level(0)` then `rtc_gpio_hold_en`, and `gpio_deep_sleep_hold_en`) to be safe. That makes BOOT useless as a wake source for that sleep, because the pin is an output.
- The BOOT button as a user button is usable: on a "N" (no-touch) variant the vendor-compatible firmware in the repo above uses it to change screens (their README). On the C board it works the same way but costs the backlight while held.

## 3. Free GPIOs, extra parts

Pin use read from the ESP32 sheet and the connector sheet:

| GPIO | Use |
|---|---|
| 0 | backlight FET gate, BOOT |
| 1, 3 | UART0 (CH340C and header P1) |
| 2 | LCD RD (the firmware holds it high) |
| 4 | LCD WR |
| 5, 18, 19, 23 | SD card (CS, CLK, MISO, MOSI), pull-ups R13 and RN2 10k |
| 12, 13, 14, 15, 25, 27, 32, 33 | LCD data DB8..DB15 (as the preset) |
| 16, 17 | LCD DC (RS), CS |
| 21, 22 | I2C SDA, SCL of the touch (4.7k pull-ups R23, R22 to 3.3 V) |
| 26 | audio amplifier input (R4 4.7k, R7 47k, C10 0.39 uF, R8 22k into the amp) |
| 34, 35, 36, 39 | no-connect on the schematic, not on any connector |

Free pins on a connector: none verified. The connectors are P1 (power-in and UART0, 4 pins), P2 (battery), P3 (speaker), the TF slot, the USB-C and the panel/touch FPCs. The CYD-family "P3/CN1 with IO35/IO22/IO27" header of the 2432S028/S032 does not exist here. The only things you can reuse are the SD pins (5/18/19/23) if the SD card is never used, reachable only on the SD slot or the module itself (soldering).

Extra parts: speaker amplifier U5 (the schematic prints SC8002B; the rzeldent list says FM8002A, same class of 8002 amplifier) on GPIO26 with a JST 1.25 2-pin speaker connector P3, SHUTDOWN/BYPASS pins strapped (amp always enabled, so it keeps drawing its quiescent current); TF/microSD slot; USB-C with CH340C. No RGB LED, no LDR/light sensor, no PSRAM drawn (verified, high confidence).

Touch connector TP-C (FPC 6-pin plus GND): CTP_SDA to IO21, CTP_SCL to IO22, CTP_RST to the net TFT_RST (EN) through R27 0 ohm, and CTP_INT (pin 5) goes through R25 0 ohm to a no-connect cross: the touch interrupt is not wired to any GPIO (verified from a zoomed crop, confidence medium-high because the cross is small; a populated 0 ohm that leads nowhere is odd but that is what is drawn). The touch reset is not a separate GPIO either: it resets whenever the ESP32 resets.

## 4. Power and an "off" mode

What is wired:

- Regulators: two AMS1117-3.3, one for the ESP32 and CH340 side, one for the panel, both from the IP5306 output (about 5 V). The AMS1117 datasheet gives a minimum load of 5 mA typical and 10 mA maximum, and quiescent current 5 mA typical, 11 mA maximum (fixed-output versions). Two of them cost about 10 mA with nothing else running. That floor cannot be removed from software. Verified from the datasheet (advanced-monolithic ds1117), applied to this board by inference.
- CH340C: powered from 3.3 V on this board (VCC and V3 pins on 3.3 V). Its idle current with no USB cable is not documented in what I found.
- Panel regulator U1 powers the panel always; the panel can only be put to sleep by command, not cut.

Sleep currents:

| Source | Number | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Bare ESP32 deep sleep (RTC timer) | about 10 uA | Espressif figure quoted by several guides |
| Typical dev board with AMS1117 + USB-serial chip in deep sleep | milliamps (one guide: 6.5 mA; another: 500 to 2000 times the datasheet value) | secondary sources, not this board |
| CYD 2432S028, "sleep below 8 mA if backlight and amp are off" | 8 mA | one unsourced claim on mischianti.org, not a measurement |
| This board, measured | not found | nobody published one |
| Estimate for this board (inferred) | about 10 to 20 mA from the battery side in deep sleep with the backlight held off (two AMS1117 at about 5 mA, CH340C, amp quiescent, IP5306 itself, plus the ESP32) | low confidence |

CST820 / CST816S touch controller (the CST820 is in the same Hynitron family; its own datasheet was not found, so I assume the 816S registers, medium confidence):

- Datasheet (Waveshare copy): typical 6 uA in standby mode, below 1 uA in sleep mode; auto standby after 2 s without touch; after a sleep command the chip is in deep sleep and returns only after a reset (reset pin, here the EN net). The fbiego driver README adds that keeping the chip in dynamic mode costs about 1.6 mA, and that register 0xFE controls auto sleep (the firmware already writes 0xFE to keep it awake, which costs that 1.6 mA).
- The sleep command register (0xA5, value 0x03) is the one commonly used in Arduino/ESP-IDF code; it is not in the datasheet I fetched, so confidence medium. The touch reset is tied to EN, so it resets on an EN/RST press or a power cycle but not on a deep-sleep wake (the EN pin does not toggle). Inferred: a chip put in sleep before a deep sleep would stay asleep after the wake with no GPIO to reset it. So do not send the sleep command; leave the chip in auto standby (about 6 uA).
- Because CTP_INT is not wired, touch cannot wake the ESP32.

ST7789 sleep: command 0x10 (SLPIN) is the standard; the panel's 3.3 V stays on, so only the panel's own sleep current remains. The datasheet figure was not looked up (not found by me). The firmware sends 0x11 at init already. After 0x11 wait 120 ms before other commands.

Wake sources available:

| Source | Possible? |
|---|---|
| Touch INT | No, not wired |
| GPIO0 (BOOT, RTC_GPIO11), ext0 low | Yes, there is an external 10k pull-up, but only if GPIO0 is not held low for the backlight (pick one); a held-low wake from deep sleep reaching the ROM bootloader with GPIO0 low could enter download mode if the button is still held at that reset (inferred) |
| Timer | Yes |
| EN/RST button | Yes (full reset) |
| IP5306 KEY button | Only when the IP5306 has shut its output off, see below |
| UART0 RX (GPIO3) | Light sleep only; not RTC |

IP5306 auto-shutdown (important for any battery "off"): the IP5306 is a power-bank IC; it turns its output off when the load stays below about 45 mA for about 32 s (community reports; spotpear forum, a troubleshooting blog and mischianti on the S032; I did not read the IP5306 datasheet, which could not be fetched). Since this board's sleep floor (about 10 to 20 mA, inferred) is under 45 mA, on battery (no USB) a deep sleep will likely end with the whole board cut off after about 32 s, and only the key button SW1 (a press) restarts it. That is a free "off": deep sleep for a minute, then the IC does the rest; but it is also a trap for "sleep and wake by timer" on battery: the timer would never fire. On USB the IP5306 passes VBUS through and stays on. All of this is inferred and untested; it should be measured before relying on it. A proper "off" for the user would be: sleep the panel (0x10), backlight off, touch left in auto standby, then `esp_deep_sleep_start()` with GPIO0 held low, and let the IP5306 cut power on battery.
