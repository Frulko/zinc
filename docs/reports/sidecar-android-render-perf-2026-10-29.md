# Sidecar on the Nexus 7 2012: where a picture's time goes and what to change (research, 2026-10-29)

Scope: read-only study of `app/sidecar/android/jni/receiver.c`, `Screen.java`, `plugins/display-rmpp/stream.h`, `app/sidecar/mac/{screen,receiver,h264,codecbench}.swift`, the Tegra 3 measurements already in `docs/reports/`, and the web. Nothing was run on the tablet and no code changed. Tags: `[measured]` = a number in this repository, `[web]` = a cited source, `[estimate]` = my arithmetic or recollection, not measured here, `[unverified]` = no source found.

Observed today (from the brief): small update 37 ms round trip (median), full 1280x800 picture 150-450 ms, 6-11 frames/s while much moves, sender encode 3-13 ms on the M1 Pro.

## 1. What the receiver does for a full picture, and where the time goes

A full picture is 1280x800 = 1.02 Mpixel, 3.07 MB of raw BGR. The receiver's serial path, per picture:

| Step (receiver.c) | Work | Estimate on a Cortex-A9 at 1.2-1.3 GHz | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| `readFull` of each RECT, 2 `read()` per RECT, plus `send16` ACK (1 `send()`, a mutex, 3 `clock_gettime`) | about 20-40 RECTs per full picture (the sender cuts runs into `workers*2` pieces, `screen.swift:465`) | well under 5 ms of CPU; the wait for bytes is the larger part | [estimate] |
| zstd (or lz4) decode into `plain` | 3.07 MB written | zstd: about 100-250 MB/s per A9 core, so 12-30 ms; lz4: about 3 times faster (2-8 ms) | [estimate]; ratio from x86 where lz4 decodes 4970 MB/s against zstd -1 1380 MB/s [web: https://github.com/lz4/lz4]. I found no published Cortex-A9 lzbench figure, so the absolute numbers need one run on the tablet |
| Sub filter plus BGR to RGBA, scalar byte loop with a running sum per channel, `decodeRect` lines 67-75 | 1.02 Mpixel, 3 loads and 4 stores, a serial dependency along x | about 8-15 ms (8-12 cycles a pixel) | [estimate] |
| `present`: `ANativeWindow_lock` (no dirty rect) then a 4 MB row-by-row `memcpy` into gralloc memory, then `unlockAndPost` | 4.1 MB read from `fb`, written to uncached/write-combined buffer | about 8-12 ms: the project measured a full-screen memmove at 5.5-6.8 ms and "Tegra 3 moves memory at about 0.5 GB/s" | [measured] `zinc-next-perf.md` ZN-743; `zinc-next-android.md` "Zero copy" |
| `lock` and `post` blocking | `dequeueBuffer` waits for SurfaceFlinger to release a buffer, tied to the 60 Hz vsync | 0-33 ms, up to two vsyncs when the queue is full | [estimate]; the project saw `present` at 14.5 ms p50 at rest (the wait for the display) [measured] |

Sum for one full picture: roughly 40-80 ms of receiver CPU, in series, on one thread, and during that time the stream thread reads nothing. That does not reach the 150-450 ms observed. So the rest is queueing and the link, not only decode:

- Pictures in flight are limited to 2 (`inFlight`), and the ACK is sent per RECT after its decode but the "picture back" time includes the wait for the lock/post (kind 12 SHOWN).
- adb over USB 2 relays through the adb server on the Mac and adbd on the tablet (two extra user-space copies each way). A rough full picture is about 77-118 KB after compression [measured, `sidecar-video-audio-research.md`] but a page switch or video frame can be several hundred KB; at 20-30 MB/s effective adb throughput [estimate; USB 2 is 480 Mbit at best] that is 10-30 ms, and TCP over adb is bursty.
- CPU starvation: the governor may keep clocks low between bursts (see section 4).

The numbers to settle this already exist on the wire and are shown in the Mac's details panel: ACK `b` (decode microseconds per RECT), kind 12 SHOWN `b` (first RECT read to all decoded) and `a` (last byte to post). Step 0 of the plan is to read them for one full-picture scene before touching code.

The 37 ms small-update round trip is the floor of this architecture: Mac capture (8-16 ms for ScreenCaptureKit) plus encode (3-13 ms) plus two adb hops plus the `lock` wait for the next vsync (average 8 ms) plus the ACK return. Decode of a small update is under 1 ms. Improving the floor means attacking the vsync wait and the capture cadence, not the decoder.

### What would gain most (receiver side), in order

| Option | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Move `lock`/`copy`/`post` to a presenter thread with a "newest picture wins" mailbox | Large for full pictures, small effort | Today the single stream thread blocks in `lock` for up to two vsyncs, so TCP backpressure builds and the RTT grows; decode of the next picture could overlap the copy of the last. Needs two `fb` (decode into one, copy from the other) or a mutex per RECT |
| Request an opaque window (`WINDOW_FORMAT_RGBX_8888`) instead of RGBA_8888 | Small effort, likely real | The layer then has no alpha, so SurfaceFlinger/HWC need not blend it against the black layer and can scan it out directly (less bandwidth, fewer GL passes). The engine already does this from API 26 (`zinc-next-android.md`, "buffer's byte order") [measured on other devices]; here the alpha 255 is already written |
| Use HAL format 5 (BGRA_8888) on this API 25 device and skip the red/blue swap | Moderate, small effort | The project found API 25 honours format 5 and that "the swapped path costs 5 ms of raster a scrolled frame" on this tablet (fling raster p50 13.2 against 8.3 ms) [measured]. The source is B,G,R already, so the loop becomes a pure 3-to-4 expansion (NEON `vld3.8` + `vst4.8`). Risk: other devices swap R/B; gate on `Build.VERSION.SDK_INT < 26` as the engine does, and put the choice in one place |
| NEON for Sub + expand | Moderate (about 10 ms of 40-80) | The running sum is a prefix sum: with `vld3` of 8 or 16 pixels, a log-step in-register prefix (3-4 `vext`/`vadd` steps) and a carry, about 3-4 times faster than the byte loop. A simpler change: have the sender use PNG's Up filter (each byte minus the byte above) for this receiver; it is a straight vector add of two rows with no serial chain. Needs one `codecbench` run to see the ratio loss against Sub (the repo says Sub: 33x against lz4 alone 18x; Up on a desktop is untested here) |
| Decode RECTs of one picture on 3 worker threads | Up to about 2.5x on the decode step if the cores are online, moderate effort | RECTs of a picture do not overlap (sender comment, receiver.swift does it "on every core"); one `ZSTD_DCtx` and one `plain` buffer per worker; the ACK order must stay increasing. Memory bandwidth (about 0.5 GB/s memmove) caps the gain, and the project saw spinning threads cost the main thread memory bandwidth [measured, `zinc-next-android.md` "CPU cores"]. Pays most with zstd (CPU-bound), little with lz4 (already near the copy rate) |
| Only copy dirty rows | Large for small updates, medium effort | A small update copies 4 MB today (8-12 ms) for a few KB of change. The trap: `ANativeWindow_lock(..., &dirty)` makes Android copy the rest from the previous buffer, a read from gralloc memory which the project found "slower still" than the write [measured]. The better scheme: lock with NULL dirty, keep a ring of dirty unions per buffer keyed by `b.bits` (3 buffers), and copy only the union of the last N pictures' dirty rows. Whether `bits` is stable per buffer on this gralloc is `[unverified]`; check with a log line first |
| Window format RGB_565 | Not recommended | Halves the bytes of the copy (4 MB to 2 MB) but needs a pack pass on the A9, `ZINC_PRESENT=565` in the engine bands visibly without dither [measured], and whether the panel is 18/24-bit is `[unverified]`. A TI report found RGB565 vs RGBA8888 changed performance by only 3% on another SoC [web: https://e2e.ti.com/support/processors-group/processors/f/processors-forum/489139/am5718-performace---rgba-8888-vs-rgb565]. The Mac's "rough" 5-6-5 frames already reduce wire bytes without changing receiver work |
| GLES 2 texture upload (`glTexSubImage2D` of dirty rects, EGLImage/gralloc) | Not recommended | The project measured `glTexSubImage2D` on this Tegra 3 at about 100 MB/s: 26-30 ms a frame into a presented texture, 7-10 ms into staging textures [measured, `zinc-next-android.md`], against 8-12 ms for the whole CPU copy. EGLImage from a gralloc buffer needs private GraphicBuffer API on Android 7 (fragile). Also the 2D software-raster-first conclusion of D119/ZN-741: the GL path is not faster here |

## 2. Hardware H.264 on Tegra 3

What is known:

- The Nexus 7 2012 decodes H.264 High profile in hardware (the OMX component is `OMX.Nvidia.h264.decode`); an XDA user reports High profile up to 40 Mbit/s at 1080p and no Hi10P [web: https://xdaforums.com/t/nexus-7-video-players-and-codecs.1769025/]. 1280x800 is half the area of 1080p, so 30 fps is safe; 60 fps is plausible but `[unverified]`. `MediaCodecList` on the device will answer through `getCapabilitiesForType("video/avc").getVideoCapabilities()` (max size, supported frame rates).
- Latency risk is the decoder's picture buffering. Moonlight's decoder errata: "some H.264 decoders add large per-frame latency with the unmodified SPS sent from NVENC; setting max_dec_frame_buffering=1 fixes it", affected devices include NVIDIA Tegra 3 and 4 and Broadcom VideoCore IV [web: https://github.com/moonlight-stream/moonlight-android/blob/master/decoder-errata.txt]. The Mac sender already rewrites the SPS with a bitstream restriction (no reordering, one picture buffered) for the Pi 3, which has the same problem (`h264.swift`, `sidecar-pi-display-latency.md` V2). That fix applies unchanged here, which is the strongest argument that H.264 can work on this tablet.
- I found no NVIDIA vendor key for low latency on Tegra 3. Moonlight's undocumented keys (`vdec-lowlatency` etc.) are for MediaTek, Amlogic, Qualcomm, Exynos [web: errata entries 16-17]. `KEY_LOW_LATENCY` needs API 30; this is API 25. Available on API 23+: `KEY_PRIORITY = 0` (real time) and `KEY_OPERATING_RATE`; both are hints that the NVIDIA OMX component may ignore `[unverified]`.
- One reported failure: an x264 `zerolatency` stream (many slices, no B-frames) did not decode on the Nexus 7 2012 while the 2013 model did [web: https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/mediacodec-failing-on-tegra-for-video-avc-stream-with-x264-zerolatency-tuning/31799]. VideoToolbox emits one slice per picture, High profile, CABAC; fine in principle, but test the first stream before building on it. If it refuses, ask VT for Main profile.
- The API: NDK `AMediaCodec` exists since API 21 with a synchronous queue (`dequeueInputBuffer`, `dequeueOutputBuffer`); the async-callback NDK API needs API 28, but the Java `MediaCodec.setCallback` works from API 21. The simplest design in this C receiver: configure `AMediaCodec` with an `ANativeWindow` from a second `SurfaceView`; the stream thread does `queueInputBuffer` with the Annex B unit; one output thread does `dequeueOutputBuffer` then `releaseOutputBuffer(render = true)`; then ACK type 10 with the decode time. That mirrors `receiver.swift` and the wire already defined (VIDEO type 10, len 0 ends it).
- Decode-to-Surface latency: no figure for this tablet. Typical hardware decoders hold 1-3 pictures; the SPS fix should make it 1. Measure with the ACK `b` for type 10 and a phone-camera test (the Pi report's method). Do not use `releaseOutputBuffer(idx, ns)` to delay; render at once.
- How others get low latency on Tegra-era Android: Moonlight (SPS rewriting, vendor keys, no jitter buffer, `releaseOutputBuffer` at once), scrcpy (reverse direction, but shows that TCP over adb plus "show the latest frame" is 35-70 ms [inference in `sidecar-video-audio-research.md`, not re-fetched]), Steam Link and Parsec (UDP, same decoder rules). Splashtop, Duet, spacedesk and Deskreen: I did not find documented Tegra 3 behaviour; treat as `[unverified]`.

Should the receiver advertise `+h264`? Yes, but only after the decoder is verified on the tablet, because the sender then turns on regions for anything that moves and a bad decoder would damage the picture. Add a runtime guard: advertise `+h264` only if `MediaCodec.createDecoderByType("video/avc")` works and a short self-decode of a stored key frame succeeds.

Should this device run `--video-all`? Not as the default. `--video-all` is marked experimental in the sender; it makes the whole screen lossy 4:2:0, which blurs coloured text. Bitrate guide: the sender uses `w*h*12` bit/s for the full screen, i.e. 12.3 Mbit/s for 1280x800 ("twice the film's, whose letters must stay sharp"). That is at the low end for desktop text with motion; 15-20 Mbit/s is trivially carried by USB 2 and within the Tegra 3 decoder's 40 Mbit/s. Best design for this tablet is the existing hybrid, and one change to it: when the whole screen moves (scrolling a page, window switch, video full screen) run the full-screen H.264 region, and once it rests 300 ms send the exact tiles over it (the machinery for rough tiles "again exact" exists). That gives smooth motion (decode about 1 frame, no 40-80 ms CPU copy, 30+ fps) and sharp text at rest, and removes the receiver's worst case (full pictures) from the CPU path. Expected: the 6-11 frames/s while much moves becomes 30 fps limited by the encoder cadence.

Cost: a second SurfaceView under the first (`setZOrderMediaOverlay`/`setZOrderOnTop` false) and a translucent RGBA surface above it with holes (alpha 0 where the video shows), as `receiver.swift` does with its plane. RGBX cannot be used for the top surface then; blending a full-screen translucent layer on Tegra 3's HWC is `[unverified]` (it may fall back to GLES composition, about 16 MB/s of fill at 60 Hz, which the Tegra 3 does at 230 Mpixel/s [measured, `low-end-gpu-perf-ideas.md`], so fine). Hide the top surface while a video-all stream is in the steady state.

## 3. Sender-side choices for a slow receiver

| Choice | Effect on this receiver | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| lz4 against zstd 1 | lz4 decodes about 3 times faster per byte [web, x86 ratio]; compresses worse (lz4 alone 18x, Sub plus zstd 33x on a desktop [measured, `stream.h`]). Over USB 2 the link is not the limit for typical frames | Default to lz4 for this receiver: the receiver chooses by leaving `+zstd` out of its hello (one word; `--lz4` exists as a flag already). Keep Sub (it is what makes lz4 compress flat areas) or switch to Up to vectorise. Verify with `codecbench`: bytes per frame for `sub + lz4` against `sub + zstd 1`, then the tablet's decode microseconds in the details panel |
| Tile size | 64 px tiles already join into row runs and are then cut into about `2 x cores` pieces. Per-RECT overhead (3 syscalls plus a mutex) is minor at 20-40 RECTs | Keep 64. If decode is made parallel, the 2x-cores piece count already fits 3-4 workers. Batch the ACKs (one per picture or every 4 RECTs) only if profiling shows the `send` in the top of `perf`/`simpleperf` |
| Rough 5-6-5 frames | They cut wire bytes (half after zstd) but not receiver work: the receiver still decodes BGR 3 bytes and expands to RGBA | Keep, for the link. It does not help CPU. A receiver-friendly rough mode would send RGB565 natively (2 bytes a pixel) so decode, Sub and copy all shrink by a third, with `+rgb565` in the hello and a 565 window format; effort moderate, gain real only if section 1's decode step dominates. Not first |
| Half resolution | Cuts decode, copy and bytes by 4 | The "x2" mode is 640x400 points captured at 1280x800 pixels, so it sends every pixel. For a "fast mode", have the receiver call `ANativeWindow_setBuffersGeometry(win, 640, 400, ...)` and announce 640x400 in the hello: SurfaceFlinger/HWC scales to the 1280x800 panel for free and the sender captures 640x400 (downscaled). Text gets soft, so use it as an option or an automatic step down while the whole screen moves, not as the default. Effort: sender capture scaling plus a hello change |
| H.264 region | See section 2 | Biggest structural gain for full-screen motion |

## 4. System tuning on the rooted device

What the repository already established:

- `nexus7-tune.sh apply` holds four cores online (cpuquiet `userspace` governor, all four written online at each boot). Measured in the engine: four held cores cut late frames of a fling from 303-366 (ROM `balanced`) to 240-297 and raster p50 from about 8.5 to 7.3-8.4 ms; one core doubles the raster [measured, `zinc-next-android.md` "CPU cores"]. Sidecar's decode is serial today, so extra cores only help once decoding is made parallel (section 1).
- The CPU governor is `intelliactive` (hotplug by cpuquiet). For a streaming session the clock between bursts matters: a long idle then a full picture meets a low clock. Try `echo performance > /sys/devices/system/cpu/cpu0/cpufreq/scaling_governor` (applies to all four on Tegra 3) or raise `scaling_min_freq` to 1000000+, for the session only. Evidence of effect on this workload: none in the repository; `[unverified]`, cheap to try, read the Mac panel's decode and RTT before and after. The engine's logs show clock p50 860 MHz in a fling, 1200-1300 in swipes [measured].
- `Window.setSustainedPerformanceMode` is not supported on this ROM [measured]; nothing to ask.
- Memory (EMC) clock: a bandwidth-bound workload (0.5 GB/s moves) may be limited by the EMC DVFS state; Tegra 3 has debugfs EMC controls (`/d/clock/emc/...`) `[unverified]`, not mentioned in any repository report. Treat as an experiment: log the clock before and during a full picture.
- GPU clocks do not matter for the CPU copy path; they would for the H.264 plus translucent overlay path (composition at 1280x800). `service call SurfaceFlinger 1008 i32 1` (disable HW overlays) was judged to have no effect for a full-screen GL app [`nexus7-2012-research.md`, own knowledge, not measured]. For Sidecar the opposite matters: keep HWC overlays on and the layer opaque (RGBX) so the single full-screen layer is scanned out without GLES composition. Check with `dumpsys SurfaceFlinger` whether the layer is "HWC" or "GLES".
- Thermal: Tegra 3 throttles when hot; the tablet charges from the Mac over the same cable while decoding and compositing, so check `/sys/class/thermal/thermal_zone*/temp` after a five-minute run. The existing HOST message (kind 10) was written for this and Android's receiver does not send it yet (type 0: temperature, busy, memory; type 1: clock now/top). Sending it is a small receiver addition that makes the Mac panel show clock and temperature during the tests above.
- Disable animations, Google services and the rest: already in `nexus7-tune.sh`.

## 5. Ranked plan

Gain is for the full-picture case (150-450 ms) unless noted; effort in agent-days of the receiver (small = under a day).

| # | Step | Expected gain | Effort | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Read what is already measured: ACK `b`, SHOWN `a` and `b` in the Mac details panel for a full picture, with `--lz4` and without; add HOST (kind 10) to the Android receiver; log `b.bits` per lock | Decides everything below; removes guesswork | small | none |
| 1 | Hello without `+zstd` for this receiver (or auto: `--lz4` when decode is the bottleneck) | Decode step 12-30 ms to 2-8 ms [estimate]; bytes up 2x, harmless over USB 2 | trivial | A fatter picture on Wi-Fi. Make it a per-transport choice |
| 2 | Presenter thread with newest-wins mailbox and an opaque RGBX (or BGRA format 5 on API under 26) window; one RECT-level mutex | Hides the `lock` vsync wait and the 8-12 ms copy behind decode; frame rate from 6-11 toward 20+ for full pictures; avoids TCP stalls | small-medium | A torn picture if the copy and decode share `fb` without a lock; use two buffers or per-RECT locking |
| 3 | NEON for the expansion (and Sub, or switch the filter to Up on the sender) | 5-10 ms of the 40-80 | medium | The prefix-sum NEON code is easy to get wrong; keep the scalar loop as reference and a T0 test comparing outputs on random data |
| 4 | Dirty-row copy with a ring of dirty unions per buffer (lock with NULL) | Small updates from about 10 ms of copy to under 1 ms; relieves memory bandwidth | medium | Depends on `bits` being stable per buffer on this gralloc; fall back to full copy if two locks return an unexpected pointer set |
| 5 | Parallel RECT decode on 3 workers (one DCtx each), ACKs in order | Up to 2.5x on the decode step if zstd stays | medium | Memory-bandwidth bound; spinning threads cost the main thread before. Skip if step 1 makes decode small |
| 6 | H.264 on the tablet: `+h264` guarded by a self-decode test; AMediaCodec to a second SurfaceView; start with the existing region logic | Full-screen motion at 30 fps with no CPU copy; hides the whole class of full pictures | medium-large | Decoder buffering (SPS fix is already in the sender), the x264-zerolatency refusal report, translucent overlay composition cost, 60 fps unverified |
| 7 | Hybrid for the whole screen: H.264 while everything moves, exact tiles when rested | Smooth motion plus sharp text; the right end state for this device | medium (sender) | Hysteresis tuning (SPICE's lesson: disable detection that mistakes UI animation for video) |
| 8 | Half-resolution fast mode through `setBuffersGeometry` and a scaled capture | 4x less of everything, blurrier | medium | Visible softness; keep optional |
| 9 | Governor `performance` or a raised min clock, EMC experiment, session script | Unknown; maybe 10-20% on bursts | trivial | Heat; make it reversible like `nexus7-tune.sh` |

Not recommended: RGB_565 window, GLES texture upload, EGLImage path (section 1 table).

If only three things are done, do steps 0, 1 and 2: they are small, independent, reversible, and the first tells whether 3-5 are needed at all. Step 6 is the largest structural win and should follow once 0-2 have been measured, since H.264 decoding on the same SoC also needs the opaque layer and the presenter design of step 2 for the tile overlay.

## 6. Open questions (could not be answered from the repository or the web)

- Absolute zstd and lz4 decode MB/s on this A9: run `lzbench` (static armv7 build) over a 3 MB captured picture on the tablet, or add a timing loop to the receiver; the Pi report's "25-35 ms for a full 1080p picture on one A53" is the only comparable [estimate].
- Whether the panel or the display controller path is 18- or 24-bit (relevant only to RGB_565 and dither).
- `OMX.Nvidia.h264.decode`: maximum frame rate at 1280x800, pictures held before output, behaviour of `KEY_PRIORITY`/`KEY_OPERATING_RATE`. Answer with a 20-line probe app and `MediaCodecList`.
- Whether SurfaceFlinger on this ROM puts the Sidecar layer on an HWC overlay or in GLES composition (`dumpsys SurfaceFlinger`).
